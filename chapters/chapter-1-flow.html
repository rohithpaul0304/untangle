03 / Mystery Box: the playable story

Inside Stage 10. Both buying and declining reach the discovery. No forced mistake.

01 · Goal

Finish with more coins

Start with 100 practice coins. Buy parts to resell.

Fox: “Can you finish with more coins?”

↓
02 · Information

Your estimate: 80

Clearly labelled “Could be wrong.” Other buyers’ estimates stay hidden.

↓
03 · Action

Choose a bid—or skip

Highest bid wins and pays its bid. Equal bids go to the earlier registered buyer.

Fox: “What will you offer?”

↓
Bid above 60

You get the box

Other bids: 40 and 60.
Example: you bid 75.

Pay 75 → sell parts for 60.
100 − 75 + 60 = 85 left.

Bid 60 or less / skip

You keep your coins

Buyer 2 gets the box for 60, then sells its parts for 60.

Your coins stay at 100.
You still see inside the box.

↓
04 · Both paths meet

Compare estimates with reality

Estimates: 40 · 60 · 80. Actual resale: 60.
Winning selected the highest estimate in this worked example.

Fox: “Winning the box and making a profit are different.”

↓
05 · Concept

Winner’s curse

When buyers estimate the same unknown value, the winner may be unusually optimistic and pay too much. It does not happen every time.

↓
06 · Changed situation

Would you pay 70 for a wheel worth 60 to resell?

For you, it replaces an equally effective 90-coin repair. Guaranteed fit; no installation cost.
Buy for 70 → save 20 versus repair.
Use the 90-coin repair → same working cart, higher cost.

Fox: “Your reason for buying is different.”

↓
07 · Everyday transfer

You got the job with the shortest estimate

Inspect the checklist. Did you find a faster method—or leave out work others counted? Compare the scope before confirming.

↓
08 · Recap and save

Winning is a result. Understanding is the lesson.

Check selected optimism. Distinguish shared resale value from personal use. Save progress → return to map or continue.
