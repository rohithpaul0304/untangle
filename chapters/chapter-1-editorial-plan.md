## The editorial decision

Stop adding compulsory concepts merely because another example is available. Use six substantial stories as the main route. Give each a different central interaction. Keep other useful examples as optional depth or later practice rather than silently deleting their source coverage.

This is not a promise to make the whole chapter short. Depth comes from meaningful decisions, explanations and transfer—not from counting scenarios. The fox helps after the learner has something to inspect or explain; it should not give the answer immediately after every tap.

## Questions within stories

Use four purposes, choosing only those appropriate to the scene:

1. **Predict before revealing:** expose the learner's current model. Never grade an uncertain historical prediction using an invented simulation.
2. **Investigate before concluding:** let the learner choose useful evidence or a question to ask. Avoid two options where one is openly foolish.
3. **Explain after seeing:** ask for the cause in their own words or through a concrete comparison. Accept uncertainty and provide the explanation without repeated failure gates.
4. **Challenge or transfer:** change a meaningful assumption or use a fresh situation. Check whether reasoning changes appropriately.

Do not insert all four at every stop. Avoid a question after every line. Mix direct scene actions, short choices, optional short explanations and comparison/replay. Text input is not mandatory after every encounter, especially on mobile.

Keep strategic choices separate from knowledge checks. A preference or risk decision can have a legitimate trade-off; explicit reasoning questions can be assessed. Record whether explanation or hints were already shown.

## Closing checks and separate practice

Retain the founder's requested end-of-chapter MCQs and a separate fresh practice quiz. The previous bank cannot be treated as ready after this edit: it asks compulsory questions about some concepts now optional.

Before building, map every required question to a taught objective on the selected route. Core objectives: interacting choices, goals and rationality, shared knowledge, strategic response, inference from selection, and comparing assumptions. Spread questions across interpretation and application instead of filling a numerical quota with definitions. Optional lessons can have optional review items.

Adaptive follow-up uses reviewed questions and explanations tied to the actual response. Do not infer mastery from opening predictions, a winning game move, speed or completion. Uncertain interpretation gets a clarifying question. Preserve the distinction between guided correction and a new independent check.

## Variety review before implementation

Lay the main route out as a sequence of verbs:

**Predict and compare → investigate motives → reveal viewpoints → reconstruct a timeline → inspect a deal → revise a plan.**

Check that these are actual different activities, not labels for the same pair of buttons followed by a speech bubble. A repeated “choose → fox explains → concept card” presentation fails this review even with different settings.

Keep the source-free learner presentation requested by the founder. Internal author notes may retain provenance. Continue using clear roles, concrete stakes and visible consequences. Keep the mascot expressive, but never use animation as the fix for weak reasoning.
