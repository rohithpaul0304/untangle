# Chapter 1 — interaction review and repair

5 October 2026. Independent critical walkthrough of the prototype against a new learner's needs. This is an author review and browser check, not an external playtest or retention finding.

## Diagnosis

The app was displaying a result paragraph while the fox continued to repeat its setup line. Two voices competed and neither followed the learner's move. The scene often remained unchanged. Shorter wording alone could not fix that mismatch.

First three encounters:

1. Hidden numbers: search-to-hiding role reversal was abrupt. After choosing a hiding number, all robot guesses arrived as one text summary. Learners could not observe the reasoning. Now their goal changes explicitly; each robot guess and higher/lower clue appears as a separate turn beside their chosen number.
2. Tournament: the old scene stacked contestants, promises, final selection and audience preferences in a paragraph. Choosing to stay had no fully displayed result. Now identity cards and two rule strips anchor the facts. The chosen branch visibly advances from today's winner to the finalist pairing to the trophy winner. The simplified fixed story is identified as such. A later real learner test must still determine whether this is too difficult as Stage 2.
3. Basketball: passing implicitly scored. The scene did not distinguish possession from shooting, and feedback could advance even after a blocked shot. Now passing moves possession; the receiving player must shoot. A defender blocks the marked player. Completion requires a basket; next scene changes which player is guarded. Learners can pass back and retry with no penalty.

## Changed feedback pattern

Chosen action → visible scene consequence → fox replies to that action → next concrete move.

Removed the green result-panel treatment. Feedback becomes an attributed conversation with the player's choice, one animated fox, and optional earlier moves. The fox is part of the response, not a second unrelated caption. Known outcomes remain authored responses; no live AI is implied.

The pictured negotiation now shows the coins assigned to both people: 1/9, 4/6, or a partner taking 7 from another deal. The old question and choice buttons collapse after a selection so the result is the focus. An explicit try-again action allows comparison. The outside option is not presented as extra coins minted into the original ten-coin pool.

Additional scenes now show explicit outcome summaries for boat order, work interruptions, campsite state, shared starting and rock–paper–scissors hands. Those remain simplified prototype scenes, not finished character animation sequences.

## Browser checks

All fourteen stages complete through their actual controls. Targeted checks cover blocked shots, pass without automatic score, teammate shot, changed defence, passing back, visible 4/6 allocation, 7-coin outside option, one fox canvas, choice echoed in conversation, no result banner, and one robot guess per action. Inspect mobile and desktop feedback layouts and preserve committed choices across exit/reload.

The interaction is more explicit; whether beginners understand and enjoy it still needs observed first-play feedback. Do not call functionality checks proof of intuitiveness.
