# Opening storyboard: “Could a ban help them?”

Previous version, preserved from the chat on 5 October 2026. Text-only proposal; not learner-tested. This is the six-screen storyboard, not the older master chapter script.

**Pacing target:** roughly 60–90 seconds, at the player’s pace. No timer.

### Screen 1 — The mystery

**Visual:** Two competing companies run television ads. Both TVs suddenly go dark.

**Headline:**
> Cigarette ads were banned from TV and radio.

**Fox, looking between the dark screens:**
> Could losing their ads actually help these companies financially?

**Button:** `Show me how`

No introduction, chapter menu, or definitions yet.

### Screen 2 — Put the player in control

**Visual:** Rewind. Two fictional companies appear: **Your company** and **Rival**. Both TVs are on.

Small, clear label: **Made-up example**

Under your company, coins arrive, then some move into an advertising bill:

**100 received − 40 on ads = 60 left**

**Fox points to your TV:**
> Customers spend the same total in this experiment. Ads help each company win customers from the other.

Then:
> Let’s try stopping only your ads.

**Action:** Tap your TV’s `Stop my ads` switch.

This is a guided experiment, not a disguised right-or-wrong question.

### Screen 3 — The catch

**Visual:** New consequence screen. Your TV switches off. Your advertising bill disappears.

Then customers move toward the rival’s still-playing TV. Your money counter changes:

**60 → 40 left**

**Fox watches the customers move:**
> You saved the ad bill. But your rival won more business. You ended up with less.

Keep the rival’s glowing TV visible. The cause stays on screen.

**Button:** `Now stop both companies’ ads`

### Screen 4 — The reversal

**Visual:** Your TV stays off. The rival’s TV now switches off too.

Customers divide evenly. Neither company pays for ads. Your counter changes:

**40 → 100 left**

**Fox points first to your unchanged switch, then to the rival’s:**
> Your ads were already off. What changed was your rival.

| Your ads | Rival’s ads | You keep |
|---|---|---:|
| Off | On | 40 |
| Off | Off | 100 |

**Button:** `Same choice. Different result.`

No confetti yet. Let the contrast carry the moment.

### Screen 5 — Give the discovery a name

**Visual:** The two outcomes remain side by side. Your identical OFF switches are highlighted.

**Fox:**
> Your result depends on your choice—and theirs.

**Concept appears:**
> **Strategic interdependence**
>
> What you get depends on what other people choose too.

**Fox:**
> That’s the first thing game theory teaches us to look for.

**Button:** `What about the real ban?`

### Screen 6 — Close the mystery honestly

**Visual:** Return to the opening television scene. The fictional coin counters disappear.

**Fox:**
> Research found signs that the ban benefited established tobacco companies. Making it harder for new brands to compete was part of the explanation.

Then:
> Our experiment shows one possible benefit of stopping together. It doesn’t prove what happened in the real world.

**Takeaway:**
> Before changing your move, ask: what can everyone else still do?

**Primary invitation:** `Try this at work`

**Secondary action:** `Pause here`

Progress saves automatically. This discovery is complete; the next scene is an invitation.

### The next scene

**Visual:** A manager receives a successful automation report.

**You:**
> This saves us five hours every week.

**Manager:**
> Keep doing it manually.

**Fox:**
> The test worked. So what’s worrying your manager?

The player investigates. A different activity begins.
