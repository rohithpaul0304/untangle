# Opening narrative: one investigation

Current playable opening, 5 October 2026. Replaces the prior company-role opening only. Other five stories unchanged.

## Throughline

“How could an advertising ban help the companies it restricts?”

Learner stays an investigator throughout. A and B are fictional companies under observation, never the learner's business. Same labels, money measure and rules across both branches. Opening prediction is acknowledged, not graded. A comparison illustrates a mechanism; historical evidence resolves the original question separately.

## Flow

Prediction → acknowledge → introduce A and B before stopping → choose which experiment first → see outcome → try the contrasting experiment → compare → name strategic interdependence → resolve opening question → real evidence → save or optional depth.

- Stop A first: 60 → 40 → 100. B stops after A.
- Stop both first: 60 → 100 → 40. B resumes while A stays off.
- Both routes establish that A's ads are off in the two compared results. Neither tells the learner they made a choice they did not make.
- Each consequence begins from the prior visual state before changing to the new state.
- No mandatory choice between pretending to be a policymaker, executive or historian.

## Teaching limits

Made-up model holds total spending fixed and excludes other costs. Historical stock-market evidence is investor expectations, not displayed company earnings. Entry-barrier explanation is retained briefly; deeper explanation and assumption check remain available. End-of-chapter questions retain interdependence and model limits.

## Review

This repairs role and causal continuity. It does not establish engagement. Evaluate both experimental orders and whether the learner can explain the difference without narration outside the app.
