# Optional alternate, not the Chapter 1 opening

Preserved from script draft 1. The cigarette case now supplies the proposed opening.

## 1. Who hid the prize?

**Source:** the book’s “Pick a Number”; the professor’s distinction between individual choice and strategic interaction. **Initial belief to examine:** choosing among boxes is the same problem whoever hid the prize.

### Author rules

Four closed boxes, one prize, one opening per round. A machine distributes the prize equally across boxes. In the second round, the learner places a visible “I will open this” marker before Mira hides the prize. Mira wants the learner to miss and must put it in a different box. She chooses equally among the other three. The learner can change their opening choice after she locks the boxes. Nothing moves after the lock. This is a new teaching model, not the book’s exact guessing game. It removes arithmetic search so attention stays on incentives and information.

### Script

**1.1 — Open something.**
- **See:** four boxes; one contains a star token. No chapter lecture or definitions.
- **Fox:** “One box holds the star. The machine has shuffled it. Open any box.”
- **Do:** open a box.
- **Then:** reveal the selected box and the prize’s location. Win: “You found it. The machine gave every box the same chance.” Miss: “Not this one. Every box had the same chance.” Neither outcome assesses understanding.

**1.2 — A person takes over.**
- **See:** Mira arrives beside the same boxes. Two small goal labels: “You: find it”; “Mira: keep it hidden.”
- **Mira:** “Show me which box you plan to open. Then I’ll hide the star.”
- **Fox:** “She wants you to miss. Place your marker.”
- **Do:** put the marker on one box.
- **Then:** Mira notices the marker, places the prize out of sight in another box, and locks all four. Show “Locked: the star cannot move.”

**1.3 — Keep the plan?**
- **Fox:** “Mira avoided your marked box. You can still change your mind. Which box will you open?”
- **Do:** open any box.
- **Then, marked box:** empty. “She used your plan when choosing where to hide.”
- **Then, different box and hit:** star appears. “You changed your plan and found it. You also needed luck: three boxes were still possible.”
- **Then, different box and miss:** reveal star elsewhere. “Changing avoided the box she ruled out. It didn’t tell you which of the other three she chose.”

**1.4 — Try the same idea without the information.**
- **See:** a fresh round; Mira locks the prize away before the marker is placed. She uses the equal-chance shuffle this time and cannot see a future marker.
- **Fox:** “This time she hid it first. Your marker cannot affect a choice she already made.”
- **Do:** place a marker, then keep it or choose another box.
- **Then:** reveal honestly. “Switching has no special advantage under this rule. What changed was the information Mira had.”

**1.5 — What did you learn so far?**
- **Fox:** “Why did changing boxes help in one round, but not the other?”
- **Do:** choose “Mira saw my plan before hiding” or “Switching always improves my chances.” Optional “Explain in my words.”
- **Response:** “The timing mattered. In the second round, she could use your plan against you. In the last round, she couldn’t.” Show this explanation after either answer.

**1.6 — Name the idea.**
- **Concept:** **Strategic interaction**.
- **Fox:** “Your result depends on your choice and someone else’s choice. Ask what they want, what they know, and when they get to act.”
- **Then:** contrast three objects: machine shuffle → no opposing goal; Mira sees marker → reacts to information; Mira locks first → cannot react to a later marker.

**1.7 — Away from the boxes.**
- **See:** two people trying to meet in a busy station with two exits, unable to message.
- **Fox:** “Your favourite exit is east. Your friend usually uses west. Picking what you like isn’t enough. What else matters?”
- **Do:** “Where my friend expects us to meet” / “Only which exit I prefer.”
- **Response:** “Meeting depends on both choices. Even knowing their usual exit may not settle it: they might head east because they expect you there.”
- **Takeaway:** “Other people are choosing with you in mind.”
- **Exit hook:** “But what if you misunderstand what they want?”

**Why this is more than guessing:** the learner compares three information structures. A lucky star is not a correct explanation. The lesson never teaches a universally safe box.
