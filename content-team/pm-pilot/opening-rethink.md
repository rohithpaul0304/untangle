# Opening rethink — The customer who wants your whole team

Status: proposed replacement opening. Founder rejected the return-report pilot as not fun. This is a short script experiment, not a validated replacement chapter or permission to build. All company events below are authored fiction. No claims about actual business outcomes.

## Why the previous draft failed

The player's job was editing a report. Choices demonstrated understanding but had little consequence for anything the player cared about. Dragging denominators was a worksheet interaction. Reviewer agreement established arithmetic correctness, not a reason to continue. Do not respond by adding confetti or making the fox louder.

## Beat 1 — The message

Visual: You lead a small scheduling app. One message slides onto a quiet product screen. Customer label: “Largest customer · ₹50,000/month.”

Customer: “We need a custom dashboard by Friday. Otherwise, we're leaving.”

Fox: “Your biggest customer is ready to walk. Your team has one week. Do you promise the dashboard?”

Two in-world replies, unscored:
- “Yes. We'll build it by Friday.”
- “Before I promise—what can't you do?”

The question is an exploratory choice, not a scored test of an unknowable business outcome. No invented lost revenue or reward is assigned to either choice.

## Beat 2 — The reply changes

If promise:
Your sent reply appears. The task board fills the team's one available week with “Custom dashboard.” No imaginary completed feature yet.
Fox: “You've promised the whole week. Your designer asks what the dashboard needs to show.”
Button: “Ask the customer” opens the customer response below.

If investigate:
Your question appears. The task board remains empty.
Customer reply appears immediately below, on a replacement scene retaining the same conversation.

Customer, both branches: “Every Friday, I open twelve schedules to see who's free. I just need one list.”

Visual: twelve schedule tabs collapse into a clear list of names and availability. This illustrates the requested task, not a feature already built.

## Beat 3 — A smaller route

Engineer: “We already have that list. It's inside each team's page. I can add a company-wide filter in one day.”

Show two build cards in the same task board:
- Custom dashboard: 5 workdays; shows who's available.
- Existing list + company filter: 1 workday; shows who's available.

Fox: “Both could show the list. Which version will you show the customer first?”

Actions: “Preview the dashboard” / “Preview the filtered list.” Both previews exist; do not mark dashboard choice morally wrong. Customer acceptance is not known before asking.

Preview response: show the selected mock-up using the same names and schedules. The learner sends “Would this solve Friday's check?” Customer: “Yes—if it covers all twelve schedules.” Highlight the coverage requirement. Explicitly retain the estimate and acceptance condition; don't imply estimates guarantee delivery.

Discovery: **Problem discovery** — “The customer asked for a dashboard. The job was finding available people. Understanding the job gave you another way to solve it.”

Promise branch adds: Fox: “You can change the plan—but first, agree on the new version with your customer.” Do not silently undo the original commitment.

Next authored beat, not built: a fresh request where the smaller feature fails a stated requirement, so “always build less” is not the taught answer. A scored check must give the time limit and acceptance requirements before asking which plan satisfies both.
