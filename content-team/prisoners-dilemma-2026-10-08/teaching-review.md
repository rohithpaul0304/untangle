## Learning design

| Check | Finding |
|---|---|
| Objective | Observable and coherent: hold the other person's action fixed, compare your alternatives, distinguish a joint change, and recheck after a changed cost. It teaches one concept; no second named framework is necessary. |
| Misconception | Specific and testable: choosing your share of a jointly desirable result does not make the other person choose theirs. The solo/joint control comparison can expose this without assuming the learner made the mistake. |
| Prediction and reveal | The rules are visible and the discovery synthesizes them, so surprise is fair. A statement choice alone does not reveal what the learner expected. Designers should embed a short expectation before the solo-switch result, such as asking what happens to your years if only you stay silent while Ravi still confesses. Keep it in the existing interaction rather than adding a separate quiz sequence. Learners who predict correctly should test and explain, not be diverted into an invented failure. |
| Changed condition | Valid: the additional campaign cost changes only the return from discounting. The resulting choice reverses for either fixed rival action. This tests the boundary of the rule rather than swapping nouns. |
| Learner action | The acceptance criteria require an actual change with visible consequences and alternatives that remain inspectable. That is stronger than selecting a definition. Candidate evidence must show the learner changes the state; prose describing an imagined switch is insufficient. |
| Application | Baseline café figures are recognition practice. The cost variant is a changed-condition probe. Neither earns a claim of transfer until observed. The takeaway is usable and avoids prescribing cooperation or competition universally. |
| Practice | Appropriate and bounded: independent causal comparisons later, worked support after two misses, then a fresh attempt. Delayed retention checks stay outside candidate scope. |

## Changed-cost arithmetic

Independently calculated each discounting café's baseline profit minus ₹2,000:

- Neither discounts: ₹5,000 / ₹5,000.
- Only you discount: ₹4,000 / ₹2,000.
- Only rival discounts: ₹2,000 / ₹4,000.
- Both discount: ₹1,000 / ₹1,000.

For each player, usual prices beat discounting by ₹1,000 at either fixed rival action. Both choosing usual prices gives the jointly preferable ₹5,000 / ₹5,000. The taught conflict is therefore removed under this new condition. Programmatic checks passed for every entry and both players' comparisons.

## Candidate checks, not new scope

1. **Load:** three tables, two objectives (fewest years / most profit), two perspectives and two kinds of switching can overload a short opening. Keep one active comparison visible; preserve names, units and the fixed person's move. Leave the complete deal accessible without narrating every author instruction. Evaluate timing in play rather than claiming the targets are already met.
2. **Obvious-answer risk:** visible amounts make individual arithmetic easy by design. The inference is why solo improvement and shared improvement disagree. A candidate that only asks “which number is better?” has not delivered the objective, even if its answer key is flawless. Elicit or construct the solo/joint explanation before the guide supplies it.
3. **Agency:** a “both switch” control belongs to an explicitly labelled teaching view; it must not imply the prisoner can make Ravi comply. Both opening choices need their actual consequences and a continuous path into the same comparison.
4. **Fair surprise:** the opening cannot secretly penalize silence, invent Ravi's move in response, or hide the campaign cost until after commitment. Keep the brief's safeguards in learner-visible wording, not only author notes.
