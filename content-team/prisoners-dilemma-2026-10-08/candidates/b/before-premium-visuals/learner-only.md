# One switch. Two rooms.

Status: Draft

## Fox setup and rules

Step into a fictional story with me. You’ll make a choice, then we’ll see what it changes.

You and your accomplice Ravi robbed a jewellery store. You’re both caught. Now you’re in separate rooms.

Your goal is the fewest prison years for yourself. Ravi wants the fewest for himself.

You choose your statement: confess and name Ravi, or stay silent and refuse the deal.

You cannot talk to Ravi or make an agreement. Neither of you sees the other’s statement before choosing. Here’s the whole deal.

If you both stay silent, you each get one year.

If you confess and Ravi stays silent, you get zero years. Ravi gets five. Zero means no prison years.

If you stay silent and Ravi confesses, you get five years. Ravi gets zero.

If you both confess, you each get three years. Let’s see what your statement changes.

Imagine Ravi stays silent. Your silence gives you one year; confessing gives you zero. What do you say?

Choices: **Stay silent** / **Confess**.

Then change only your own statement.

Imagine both confessing. Ravi keeps confessing. If only you become silent, will your years **fall**, **stay**, or **rise**?

Imagined comparisons from both confessing: **Imagine only Ravi stays silent** / **Imagine both stay silent**.

## Café setup and rules

Now you run a café near another owner’s café. In this made-up example, you want the most profit today—money left after costs.

You each choose a discount or usual prices, without seeing the other’s choice. Only today counts. All costs are included. Here are the four results.

If both cafés keep usual prices, you each make ₹5,000.

If you discount and the other café keeps usual prices, you make ₹6,000. They make ₹2,000.

If you keep usual prices and the other café discounts, you make ₹2,000. They make ₹6,000.

If both cafés discount, you each make ₹3,000.

Imagine you both discount: ₹3,000 each. The other café keeps discounting. Change only your action: usual prices or a discount?

Choices: **Keep usual prices** / **Run a discount**.

## New cost setup and rules

Now imagine each discount costs an extra ₹2,000 today. Usual prices cost nothing extra. Everything else stays the same.

With the new cost, both keeping usual prices still earns ₹5,000 each.

If you discount and they keep usual prices, your ₹6,000 minus the ₹2,000 cost leaves ₹4,000. They still make ₹2,000.

If you keep usual prices and they discount, you still make ₹2,000. They make ₹4,000 after the cost.

If both discount, each makes ₹3,000 minus ₹2,000: ₹1,000 each. Those are all the new profits.

Imagine you both discount: ₹1,000 each after the new cost. The other café keeps discounting. What do you choose?

Choices: **Keep usual prices** / **Run a discount**.

What shaped your choice this time? You can leave a note or skip it. I won’t judge what you write.

Optional input: write a thought or leave blank.
