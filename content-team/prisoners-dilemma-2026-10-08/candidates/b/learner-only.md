# One switch. Two rooms.

Status: Draft

## Fox setup and rules

Come try a fictional story with me. You make the choices; we’ll see what happens.

You and your accomplice Ravi robbed a jewellery store. You’ve been caught—and put in separate rooms.

You want the fewest prison years for yourself. Ravi wants the fewest for himself.

Accept the deal: confess and name Ravi. Or refuse it: stay silent. You control only your statement.

No talking or agreements with Ravi. You each choose without seeing the other’s statement. Here’s the deal.

If you both stay silent, you each get one year.

You confess; Ravi stays silent. You get zero prison years—none. Ravi gets five.

You stay silent; Ravi confesses. You get five years. Ravi gets zero.

If you both confess, you each get three years. Ready to try it?

Imagine Ravi stays silent. Silence gets you one year; confession gets you zero. What do you say?

Choices: **Stay silent** / **Confess**.

Then change only your own statement.

Imagine both confessing: three years each. Ravi keeps confessing. If only you stay silent, what happens to your years?

Choices: **Fall** / **Stay** / **Rise**.

Imagined comparisons from both confessing: **Imagine only Ravi stays silent** / **Imagine both stay silent**.

## Café setup and rules

Now imagine running a café beside another owner’s. Your goal: the most profit today—money left after costs.

Choose discounts or usual prices without seeing the other’s choice. Only today counts; all costs are included.

When both cafés keep usual prices, you each make ₹5,000.

You discount; they keep usual prices. You make ₹6,000. They make ₹2,000.

You keep usual prices; they discount. You make ₹2,000. They make ₹6,000.

When both cafés discount, you each make ₹3,000.

Imagine both discounting: ₹3,000 each. They keep discounting. What will your café do?

Choices: **Keep usual prices** / **Run a discount**.

## New cost setup and rules

One change: every discount now costs an extra ₹2,000. Usual prices cost nothing extra. Everything else stays unchanged.

Both keeping usual prices means nothing extra to pay. You still make ₹5,000 each.

You discount; they keep usual prices. Your ₹6,000 minus ₹2,000 leaves ₹4,000. They still earn ₹2,000.

You keep usual prices; they discount. You earn ₹2,000. After the extra cost, they earn ₹4,000.

Both discounting means ₹3,000 minus ₹2,000: ₹1,000 each. That’s after the extra cost.

Imagine both discounting: ₹1,000 each after the extra cost. They keep discounting. What will you do?

Choices: **Keep usual prices** / **Run a discount**.
