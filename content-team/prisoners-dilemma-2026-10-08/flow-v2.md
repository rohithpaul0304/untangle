## Act 1 — The rooms (one-shot)

**S01 · The job.** Art: the jewellery store at night, glass on the pavement. Fox (surprised): "You and Ravi just robbed a jewellery store. Sirens." Button: **Run.**

**S02 · Caught.** (tap 1) Art: the two rooms. Ravi's face: scared. Fox (thinking): "Caught. Two rooms. You can't talk to him and he can't talk to you." Button: **The door opens.**

**S03 · The deal.** (tap 2) The inspector speaks in his own bubble, not the fox's: "Name Ravi while he stays quiet, and you walk out today; he gets five years. Stay quiet while he names you, and you get five; he walks. Both quiet: one year each. Both talk: three each. He's getting the same offer." The deal sheet lies on the table as a prop, a four-cell card the learner can glance at from now on. Fox (one line, small): "You want the fewest years. So does he." **Decision:** Stay silent / Name Ravi. Real uncertainty: nobody has told the learner what Ravi will do.

**S04 · Ravi talked.** (tap 3, first consequence) The inspector returns: "Ravi talked." Ravi through the glass: *ashamed, can't meet your eyes* if you stayed silent; *cold* if you also talked. Counters land: You 5 · Ravi 0, or You 3 · Ravi 3. Fox, silent branch (sad): "You stayed silent. Ravi named you. Five years for you; none for him." Fox, talk branch (thinking): "You named Ravi. He named you. Three years each." Button: **Why did he do it?** Ravi’s statement is authored as “Name you” before the learner chooses, regardless of their selection. It is never selected in reaction to that choice. This first statement is unscored: the deal is visible, Ravi’s statement is not. No earlier pact or promise is implied.

**S05 · His chair.** (tap 4) Art: Ravi's room, the inspector with him, Ravi's face calculating. Fox: "Same offer, from his chair. If you stayed quiet, talking got him zero instead of one. If you talked, three instead of five. Either way, talking cut his prison years." The deal sheet highlights Ravi's two rows as he speaks. Button: **And for me?**

**S06 · Your chair, and the name.** (tap 5) The deal sheet highlights your rows. Fox: "Same for you. Whatever Ravi did, talking cut your years. The same pressure on both of you." The both-quiet cell glows. "One year each was on the table. Neither of you could reach it alone." Concept card: **Prisoner's dilemma** — when each person’s better move, holding the other’s move fixed, leaves both worse off than the other shared outcome if both choose it. Button: **Is it always like this?**

No "imagine" anywhere. Both learner branches reach S05 and S06 unchanged; the sting differs, the teaching does not.

## Act 2 — Tomorrow exists, until day ten (the loop)

**S07 · A different life.** (tap 6) Explicit time jump. Art: two cafés. Fox: "Different life. You run a café. Meera runs the one next door. For ten days, you both set today's price each morning. You'll see her again—until the ten days end." Meera's face: friendly wave. Button: **Open the café.**

**S08 · The board.** (tap 7) The profit board, one screen, the same four-cell shape as the deal sheet: both usual ₹5,000 each; you discount and she doesn't ₹6,000 / ₹2,000; she discounts and you don't ₹2,000 / ₹6,000; both discount ₹3,000 each. Fox: "Same shape as the two rooms. Ten days. Aim for the most total profit for your café. Meera remembers yesterday." **Day 1 decision:** Usual prices / Discount.

**Days 1–10.** (taps 8–17) One tap per day. After each choice the day resolves at once: both awnings show today's price (a SALE sign on whoever discounted), today's profit lands in two counters, running totals tick up, a ten-day strip at the top fills in (two rows of dots, yours and hers), and Meera's face changes. Author rule: Meera is a programmed character, not a forecast of real people. She starts at usual prices; on each later day her action copies the learner’s previous-day action, regardless of today’s choice. This fixed rule is authored before play. The opening need not reveal the algorithm; "she remembers yesterday" introduces something to investigate. Discovery choices are unscored. Her next move is never invented to reward or punish the learner. Her faces: *friendly* after a day you both kept usual; *hurt* the morning after you discounted on her; *hard* while she discounts; *thawing* the first day she goes back to usual; *grim* when you both discount. The fox has four short day lines, used by outcome, never a lecture: "Six for you, two for her." / "She copied yesterday’s discount. Three each." / "Two for you. Your usual prices; her discount." / "Five and five. Same again tomorrow?" The tomorrow line is allowed only on days 1–9. On day 10, both-usual instead gets "Five each. That’s the tenth and final day." If the learner discounts on day 10 after usual-price days, the fox: "Last day. Your discount earns six; she earns two. There’s no next day here." Other day-ten outcomes use their factual profit line without a tomorrow prompt. This makes the finite ending visible without inventing the learner’s reason for choosing.

**S19 · Ten days.** (tap 18) The full strip and both totals. The fox narrates what actually happened, from the strip, in two lines ("Day 1 you discounted. Day 2 she copied you. Day 3 you went back; day 4 so did she."). Then the honest benchmark, as a fact about the board rather than an imagined run: "Ten days at usual prices would have been ₹50,000 each." Then distinguish that shared benchmark from the learner’s own maximum: "Against Meera’s rule, nine usual-price days then a last-day discount earns you ₹51,000 and her ₹47,000. That’s your highest possible total here." Buttons: **Run the ten days again** / **Stop here.**

**Second run (optional, same ten taps).** Same Meera, same rule, fresh totals. At the end both strips sit side by side with both totals. The comparison the learner needs is between two things they did, not between two things they imagined.

**S20 · The takeaway.** Fox: "In the rooms, talking cut your years either way. With Meera, your price sets her next price—until day ten. The last day has no next move." Then: "Against her rule, nine usual-price days and one final discount earn you the most. That result depends on her rule and the stopping point." Then the work line: "Before choosing, ask: could today change their next move—and is there a next move?" Stop with saved progress.

## Worked numbers for the authors (not learner copy)

These are results of the authored copy-previous-day rule, with Meera starting at usual prices; they are not predictions about a human café owner. Always usual: ₹50,000 each. Always discount: ₹6,000 then nine days at ₹3,000 = ₹33,000 for you, ₹29,000 for her. Discount on day 1 only: ₹6,000, ₹2,000, then eight days at ₹5,000 = ₹48,000 each; that day-one discount lowers each ten-day total by ₹2,000 versus always usual. Alternate every day, starting with a discount: ₹40,000 each. Discount on day 10 only: ₹51,000 for you, ₹47,000 for her. Enumerating all 1,024 possible ten-day learner sequences confirms ₹51,000 is the maximum own total, reached only by nine usual-price days followed by a day-ten discount; Meera then totals ₹47,000. Each path is a sum of board cells, but experimenting does not guarantee visiting all four. Always usual visits only usual/usual; always discount visits discount/usual and discount/discount. Replay permits further exploration without guaranteeing full coverage. This arithmetic check is not a built-deck or human-play result.

Author boundary for performance: Ravi’s and Meera’s expressions dramatize the written outcomes. They are not evidence of actual motives, loyalty, a broken promise, or why the learner chose an action. Meera’s expressions never change her programmed pricing rule. Do not grade the learner’s values from either character’s face.
