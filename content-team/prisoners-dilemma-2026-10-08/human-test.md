# Prisoner’s dilemma — next play test

Status: Draft. Protocol prepared; human tests completed: 0.

## Founder review first

Play the repaired draft from index.html, preferably on a phone-sized screen. Do not read script.md beforehand. At the end, record:

1. Where did you want to see what happened next?
2. Which choice felt like an obvious quiz answer?
3. Which explanation helped you understand something you had missed?
4. Where did the story, control or number change stop making sense?
5. Continue with this interaction, revise one specific part, or discard it?

Founder judgment determines direction, but cannot waive a known factual defect. This is a product review, not unfamiliar-learner evidence.

## Unfamiliar learner test, after selection and Quality checks

Invite three people who have not seen this draft. Use participant labels, not personal information. Show only the selected learner deck. Do not coach during play or explain the intended insight. Record where they pause, their exact questions, choices, requests for help, and whether they voluntarily continue.

After play ask: “Why might both people end up with three years even though one year each is available?” Then: “What happens if only you change your statement while Ravi still confesses?” Ask what changed their café decision when the additional cost appeared. Record their own words before discussing answers. Do not infer understanding from choosing a displayed larger or smaller number.

A fresh application item must be authored and independently checked after selection; do not claim that repeating the deck’s café table is independent transfer. A delayed recall check is separate and must use a pre-agreed delay and fresh question. No retention claim from the same session.

## Evidence limits

Completion is navigation evidence. Correct answers may reflect arithmetic or copying. Voluntary continuation is an engagement observation, not proof of learning. Three people can expose defects, not establish broad demand or prove retention. No pass rate has been observed yet.
