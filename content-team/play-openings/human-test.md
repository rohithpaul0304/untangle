# Three-opening test protocol

Status: prepared, not run. Participants: zero. Independent AI review is recorded separately.

Show learner-facing scripts only, one screen at a time; never show author rules, intended lessons or evaluator conclusions. A paper walkthrough tests interpretation, not animation quality. Explain any toy simulation rules exactly as written. Do not supply missing explanations.

Compare each candidate with unfamiliar target learners. Vary presentation order across people so the first or last candidate does not always benefit. Keep starting instructions consistent. Capture choices and explanation before revealing consequences.

For each opening:
1. Show first screen. Ask “What can you do here?” If essential information is missing, record the confusion rather than patch it verbally.
2. Let the person choose. Show that branch's authored result. Ask “What changed, and why?”
3. Show the second situation without coaching. Record action and reasoning before feedback.
4. Offer a real stopping point and the option to continue; do not urge continuation. Record what they choose.

After all three, ask “Which would you choose to keep playing, and where did you lose interest?” Keep exact words. Do not ask whether our favorite is fun or explain why it should be.

Report comprehension and preference separately. Understanding the intended rule does not establish enjoyment; liking a character does not establish learning. A tiny convenience sample gives directions for revision, not population retention estimates. No numerical fun score or claimed dopamine measure.

If no unfamiliar person is available, retain the pending status. AI reviewers may trace choices and identify confusing rules; never substitute their preferences for participant responses.
