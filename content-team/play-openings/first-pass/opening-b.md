# Opening B — The queue moved

**Candidate, not approved.** About one minute is a target, not a timed result. Original fictional product model; no claim about real streaming systems. Learning objective: improve the limiting step in an entire user journey, and recheck when conditions change. Concept: **bottleneck**. No code, human test, or verified motion.

## 1. First screen — exact learner view

Fox: “You run a concert app. Six fans are waiting. Get as many as you can into the show.”

Compact scene, left to right: six distinct fan tokens → **Ticket check: 4 places** → **Watch live: 2 places** → concert stage.

One upgrade tile below: **Add 2 places**. Two empty sockets, one on each step.

Fox: “Drop your upgrade onto either step. Then open the doors.”

Visible rules, together beside the controls:
- “Each fan needs a place at BOTH steps.”
- “In this round, each place admits one fan. Places do not refill.”
- “One upgrade. It adds 2 places to the step you choose.”

Drag tile to a socket, or tap the tile then its destination. Tap **Open doors** to commit. No countdown. The show waits for this action. Doors remain labelled with their current capacities; preview updates immediately.

## 2. First consequences — conditional, never shown upfront

**If Ticket check:** capacities become 6 and 2. Six fans pass ticket check; two enter the concert. Four remain visibly queued immediately before Watch live. Settle at **Watching: 2 of 6**.

Fox: “All six got past tickets. Four are still stuck outside the show.”

**If Watch live:** capacities become 4 and 4. Four fans pass both steps into the concert. Two remain before Ticket check. Settle at **Watching: 4 of 6**.

Fox: “Four reached the show. Now the two outside are waiting at tickets.”

In either branch, fox points to the actual remaining queue, not an abstract chart. Final positions and counts persist. Reduced-motion version replaces moving tokens with the same labelled final positions.

Both branches then share the same button: **Move my upgrade**. This returns the same six fans to the entrance and moves the tile to the other step automatically. Caption: **Replay: same fans, other upgrade**. The other branch runs, with its exact corresponding fox line. Keep two tiny comparison badges: **Tickets +2: 2 watching** / **Live +2: 4 watching**.

## 3. Discovery, on that consequence screen

Fox: “Why did moving the same upgrade let two more people in?”

Optional one-line response; **Show me** advances without requiring writing. No grading of this reflection.

Fox: “Extra ticket places only moved the queue. Extra live places let more fans finish.”

Visible concept: **Bottleneck — the step that limits how many people finish.**

Fox: “Improve the whole journey, not just the busiest-looking part.”

Button: **Next show**.

## 4. Second move — changed condition

Same app, same six fans, same upgrade tile, reset to the tray. Before choosing, display **Ticket check: 2 places** and **Watch live: 4 places**.

Fox: “Next show. Live has four places now, but ticket checks have only two. Where does your upgrade go?”

Same visible rules, controls, and **Open doors** button. The concept name remains small and visible.

**If Ticket check:** 4 and 4. Four enter; two wait before tickets. **Watching: 4 of 6**.

Fox: “Four in. You found the limit again—even though it moved.”

**If Watch live:** 2 and 6. Two enter; four wait before tickets. Four live places remain visibly empty. **Watching: 2 of 6**.

Fox: “Four empty live places. The fans can’t reach them. Move the upgrade to tickets.”

Button **Move to tickets** resets this round and runs the 4-and-4 result above. No restart.

## 5. Stop

Fox: “Before building more, find where people stop reaching the result they came for.”

**Opening complete.** Button: **Stop here**. Teaser, not another required exercise: “Next: what if the queue disappears because people give up?”

## Author check

All outcomes follow min(6, ticket places, live places): first 6/2→2 or 4/4→4; second 4/4→4 or 2/6→2. No invented response after selection. Two independent upgrade placements, one automatic comparison; roughly 9–11 taps plus two drags. Corrections applied: stable role, rules before choices, specific branch acknowledgment, queues visibly reconcile, named discovery, fresh changed condition, no report editing or rewards. Main risk: two small capacities may feel like an obvious arithmetic puzzle; the script cannot establish fun. Next review should test whether someone explains why unused capacity did not help, then voluntarily continues.
