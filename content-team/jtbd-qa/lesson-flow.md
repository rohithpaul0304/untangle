# JTBD: two scenarios → guided Q&A → quiz

Status: Draft · text only · v0.2

## Two situations

### Scenario 1 · A milkshake before work
A fast-food restaurant sees people buying milkshakes early in the morning. Why a milkshake then?

Commuters wanted something that would keep hunger away and make a boring drive less boring. It needed to last and be easy to manage with one hand.

That purpose points toward a lasting drink and fast checkout before work.

### Scenario 2 · A film tonight—or something worth finding?
Now move to the era of DVDs delivered by post. Netflix and Blockbuster both helped people watch films, but consider two different situations.

Someone planning ahead wants help finding a film they will enjoy and watching it on their own schedule. Netflix's DVD-by-mail service could fit. Someone choosing a film for tonight needs a copy immediately. A nearby Blockbuster with the film in stock could fit.

The deadline changes what works.

### The idea: Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)
A **job to be done** is the purpose someone wants a solution to serve in a particular situation.

For the commuter, “buy a milkshake” is the activity. Getting through the drive with less hunger and boredom is the purpose. One-handed use is a requirement shaped by the drive.

Start with: **“When ___, I want to ___.”** Use what people say and do. A product choice alone cannot tell you every motive.

## Guided Q&A
Choose an answer first. You can add a sentence explaining why. Then the guide responds on the next screen.

### Guided question 1 · What is the commuter's job?
Think back to the commuter. Which answer names the purpose, rather than the purchase or a product quality?

A. Buy a milkshake before starting the morning drive.
B. Get through the drive with less hunger and boredom.
C. Have a thick drink that fits in one hand.

**Guide response after choosing B:** “Correct. This names what the commuter wants the milkshake to do during the drive.”

### Guided question 2 · What changed for the film customer?
A customer usually plans films ahead. Today they say, “Friends are arriving in an hour. I need a film we can watch tonight.” Which job is supported now?

A. Discover less familiar films for future evenings at home.
B. Impress visiting friends with a more unusual film collection.
C. Get a film ready for the gathering starting tonight.

**Guide response after choosing C:** “Correct. The new deadline makes having a film ready tonight the relevant purpose.”

## Quiz · Identify the job
For each situation, choose the purpose best supported by the facts. Use the evidence given; you do not need to know these products.

### Q1 · Same delivery app, different evening
Last week, Leena used a meal-delivery app because she was too tired to cook after work. Today she says, “My parents are visiting. I have time to cook, but I want to sit and talk with them instead.” She orders again. What is today's job?

A. Spend the visit talking together without preparing the meal.
B. Recover after a tiring workday without preparing the meal.
C. Give her parents a special treat from a favourite restaurant.

### Q2 · A new dashboard
You're speaking with a team leader about a work dashboard. They say, “I need to see which orders are late and assign someone to follow up before our Monday meeting.” Which job can you support from this evidence?

A. Reassure senior leaders that the team's performance is improving.
B. Prepare follow-up work for late orders before the weekly meeting.
C. Reduce the team's anxiety about discussing delays in the meeting.

### Q3 · Why record the call?
You're researching a call-recording tool. A customer says, “The engineer who will fix this cannot join the call. I need them to hear the fault description afterwards without making the customer explain it again.” Which is the job?

A. Save an audio file of the customer call for later playback.
B. Create a searchable recording with clear sound for the engineer.
C. Give the absent engineer the details needed to investigate the fault.

## Feedback flow

After each submitted answer, the guide explains why it fits—or which part of the situation it misses. A wrong answer can open a fresh practice question before returning to the original with the selection cleared. If the two fresh practice questions have already been used, offer a worked explanation and one supported retry instead of recycling a seen answer as new evidence. Repeated difficulty ends with explained support and a choice to pause or continue with the concept marked for review. Never restart the chapter.

This is a text draft. Scenarios remain provisional. The complete choice-by-choice feedback and extra practice are in the separate answer key and learner packet. No app behaviour has been implemented.
