# Would you launch the slower rental service?

Status: Draft

**Exact hook:** “You’re designing a DVD rental service in the early 2000s. Your rival lets people collect a film today. Yours sends it by post. What would make the wait worth it?”

**Consequential choice:** Choose the promise the service will be built to keep:

- “Help people get tonight's new release.”
- “Help people find films they'll want to keep discovering.”

The player commits effort to either nearby stores, shelf space and new-release stock, or a taste-matched catalogue, postal delivery and viewing without return deadlines. Both require work; neither delivers everything.

**Visible comparison:** Selecting the first assembles a store shelf beside a ‘tonight’ calendar space. A film moves from shelf to home; the catalogue remains bounded by what that store stocks. Selecting the second assembles a personal film queue and a postal route. A film travels toward a later evening; its return deadline disappears. The postal wait remains visible. The unselected direction stays beside it, making the sacrificed capability explicit. No customer cheers, revenue changes or winner appears.

**Earned JTBD discovery:** ‘Slower film rental’ assumes both services are being hired to solve tonight. Once the purpose becomes discovering films suited to your taste and watching on your schedule, speed to tonight stops being the sole comparison. Jobs to Be Done means comparing services against the progress people seek in a particular situation. A disadvantage can remain real while becoming less decisive.

**Reason to continue:** Can you describe the situation in which the direction you rejected would be the better hire?

**Author boundary:** Historical contrast from the supplied HBS account; design choice and visual reconstruction are teaching devices. No claim that these were universal customers, exclusive capabilities, or the cause of either company's fate.
