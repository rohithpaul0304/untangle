# C — The house is ready. Their life isn't.

Status: Draft

**Historical anchor.** A Detroit-area builder attracted visits but few purchases. Requested bay windows did not improve sales. Buyers struggled to decide what to do with their dining tables. The eventual offer included moving help, storage, a sorting room and design changes.

**Exact hook.** “You sell new homes. Your customers like them. But an old dining table keeps winning.”

**First decision — fictional adaptation.** You run the development. A couple wants less housework and likes your smaller home. Their family furniture will not all fit; they haven't decided what to keep. You can offer either:

- **“Make room for their table.”**
- **“Store their things while they settle.”**

Preserve familiar life inside the new home, or separate moving day from permanent decisions. This is an unscored choice; neither guarantees a sale.

**Visible consequences.** *Make room:* the table fits in the revised floor plan; the other undecided belongings remain at the old house. You solved a space problem, but several decisions still block packing. *Store things:* the belongings move into a labelled temporary store and the new home can be occupied without discarding them first. Sorting remains unfinished; storage postpones decisions rather than erasing them. These are stated mechanics of the fictional offers, not historical branch outcomes.

**Earned insight.** Jobs to be done means the progress someone needs in their circumstances. The job can include making the transition possible, not merely owning the destination. Ask what people must give up or reorganize before your product becomes usable.

**Reason to continue.** What happens when storage ends? Can your offer help people complete the move rather than merely delay its hardest part?

**Risk.** The table may feel like a contrived clue. The real puzzle—desirable homes still not selling—must lead; the emotional explanation cannot become a sentimental lecture.

## Source and verification notes

Verified accessible primary institutional account: https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/ — section “Condominium sales,” read 7 October 2026.

Supported historical claims: Detroit-area builder; many visits/few sales; bay windows did not improve sales; buyers' dining-table decisions; table represented family; larger dining rooms; moving services; two years of storage; sorting room. The source attributes the investigation to Bob Moesta. It reports price and sales changes, deliberately omitted from the premise. It does not establish which change independently caused any result.

The source presents these services and design changes together, not as an either/or historical experiment. Our two offers, couple, floor-plan mechanics and consequences are an explicit fictional adaptation. No claim is made that storage guaranteed a sale or that a larger dining room failed historically.

Also accessed https://hbr.org/podcast/2016/12/the-jobs-to-be-done-theory-of-innovation — accessible Christensen interview, but not used to substantiate the housing case. No paywall or CAPTCHA bypass used.
