# Food/commute premise: the broken milkshake machine
Status: Draft

**Recommendation: reject this pool for the next opening unless the broader comparison is weaker.** This is the strongest premise I can defend from the available intake, but it risks repeating the founder’s rejected feature-fit exercise.

**Source facts.** The supplied HBS intake describes commuters using milkshakes for hunger relief and something that lasts through their drive. Checkout speed and drink consistency matter in that circumstance. It does not establish sales changes, a named chain’s results, demand for replacement products, or a broken-machine incident. Everything playable below is explicitly a fictional café exercise inspired by that account.

**Exact opening line.** “You run a café. Your milkshake machine has broken, and the morning commuters are arriving. What will you sell them instead?”

**First choice.** Put one replacement on the counter: **“A coffee and banana”** or **“A thick bottled smoothie.”** Both are familiar, carryable breakfast offers. Coffee plus fruit offers familiar breakfast components; a smoothie keeps the drink format. No score: the learner has not yet seen what these particular commuters need. The café controls the replacement offer, not whether customers buy it.

**Visible consequence for each choice.** Follow the chosen offer through one explicitly fictional, prewritten commuter journey, with the unused alternative available for comparison afterward. The journey and object behavior must be fixed before selection; show neither sales totals nor customer approval as an invented business result.

- **Coffee and banana:** The player places two objects into the commuter’s available cup holder and bag. At a parked stop, the banana is eaten; the peel remains, coffee remains, and the rest of the journey is still ahead. The offer has supplied food and a drink, but the timeline exposes that eating ended early. Acknowledge: “You replaced breakfast. The drive isn’t over.” This observation is a clue, not proof that the offer failed.
- **Thick bottled smoothie:** One object occupies the holder. The commuter takes successive sips along the same journey; food and drink remain together. Acknowledge: “Your replacement stays with the drive.” The final state establishes only its fit for this scripted situation, not commercial demand or superior nutrition.

**Earned JTBD discovery.** The milkshake’s competitive set includes breakfasts and things that occupy a journey, not only other milkshakes. Jobs to be Done means understanding the progress someone seeks in their situation; that can change which products you regard as substitutes. The evidence should invite investigating this possibility, not claim every commuter has the same job.

**Why continue.** The next customer has five minutes before boarding a train. The same slow drink may become a liability. The question becomes whether to keep one replacement for everyone or change the offer by circumstance.

**Failure risk.** Strong and material: the smoothie may look like the obvious milkshake substitute from the start. Packaging and duration can become a sorting worksheet. The intake cannot support a surprising commercial consequence without additional evidence. I would not turn this into a deck merely to fill a third slot; seek a richer documented food case if food is essential.
