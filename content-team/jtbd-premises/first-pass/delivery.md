# C — The meal that gets in the way

Status: Draft

**Source facts.** The supplied DoorDash passage describes a service people use to satisfy hunger without leaving home. That is the only company claim used. The situation below is fictional; prices, times and people are authored conditions, not DoorDash research or an experiment. PayPal was weaker here: with only ease and trust available, its opening kept collapsing into “make payment feel safer.”

**Exact hook.** “You're hosting four friends for dinner. The food is ready across the road. Do you bring dinner here—or move everyone to dinner?”

**Situation and first decision.** You're the host. Everyone is hungry, and the evening has finally got going: two friends are midway through telling the group how they met. You promised to cover dinner. The restaurant has your food ready and a table available. Staying means a £6 delivery charge and a quoted 25-minute wait; walking over means food in five minutes and interrupting the conversation to move. These conditions are visible before the choice. There is no timer or scored answer.

Two actions:

- **“Have dinner brought here.”**
- **“Take everyone to dinner.”**

Both protect a legitimate part of hosting: keep the group comfortable and together, or feed them sooner and save money. Neither is a disguised test of good manners.

**Visible consequences.**

*Bring it here:* the restaurant's ready meals remain outside the home while the group stays around your table; your dinner spending increases by £6. The conversation continues in the same place. The scene advances 25 minutes and the meals arrive. Hunger lasted longer; the gathering wasn't interrupted. Nobody declares this the better evening.

*Move everyone:* the group gathers coats and leaves the table; the unfinished story moves with them as fragments of conversation on the walk. Five minutes later, they sit with their meals. The £6 stays in your budget. You fed everyone sooner; you changed the setting and broke the original conversation's flow. Nobody suddenly becomes angry to punish the choice.

**Useful insight the action could earn.** Jobs to be done: what someone is trying to accomplish in a particular situation. Here the purchase sits inside “host an evening,” not merely “obtain food.” Delivery competes with moving the gathering, not just with another delivery app. At work, examine what people rearrange or abandon to use your product; the alternative may sit outside your product category.

**Reason to continue.** The same friends meet again, but this time the point is to get the host out of the house after a week indoors. Does the convenient option still help? The changed purpose should change what delivery is worth, without changing its features.

**Why it might still be boring — recommendation.** This has a consequential choice but a weak hook. Most people already understand paying to avoid interrupting an evening, and the insight risks being obvious feature matching. I would **not recommend building this opening**. Keep only the idea that a product competes with rearranging the surrounding activity; this source pool does not yet supply a sufficiently puzzling real-world event.
