# Newcomer test

Status: Draft. Human newcomer tests: 0.

Run only after the opening clears the recorded design gate. Invite someone who has not seen this project. Give them the deck, not the script or answer key. Do not explain JTBD first.

## Before play
Say: “Try this on your own. You can stop whenever you want. Tell me when something makes no sense.” Do not ask whether they find it fun before they play.

## Observe without coaching
Record the first point they hesitate, the choice they make, what they expect to change, what they notice changing, and any unexpected scrolling or missed controls. Record exact words, not interpretations. Separate choices about the product from navigation taps. If help is necessary, record the exact help; the remaining attempt is supported.

## After the second move
Ask one question at a time:
1. “What did you change, and why?”
2. “Why could the same customer want one part faster and another part slower?”
3. “What would you need to learn before offering this to someone else?”
4. “Would you keep playing, or stop here? What made you choose that?”

Then show the provisional practice packet one screen at a time. Hide feedback until each choice is committed. Ask for the reason before revealing feedback. Look for reasons based on behaviour, not matching words. For the incomplete-evidence item, record whether they recognise what remains unknown. For the product decision, record whether they connect their offer to the current job.

## What counts
Understanding: the person connects circumstances → wanted progress → product change without repeating the guide or receiving a hint. A correct letter alone does not count.
Transfer: their explanation uses the changed condition to change the offer; it is not a memorised “thicker is better” rule. This immediate test does not establish retention.
Interest: record their voluntary choice and stated reason. Do not infer enjoyment from completion or one enthusiastic word.

## Decision
If they cannot explain why the result happened, send the relevant scene back to its author. If they solve by matching wording, send the item back. If they understand but choose to stop from boredom, revisit the play mechanism. Do not fix this by adding rewards or more text. Keep every observed failure in the report.
