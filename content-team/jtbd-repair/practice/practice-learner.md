## Two situations

### FILM-A · Planning ahead
Fox: “You're helping Arun choose a film service. It's Monday. His next film night is Saturday.”

Netflix's DVD-by-mail service and Blockbuster's rental shops were real. This is a simplified historical setting; Arun and his choices are invented. There is no streaming in this scene.

Arun has watched everything by a director he likes. He makes a list of three unfamiliar films by other directors. None is in the nearby shop. All three can arrive by post before Saturday. The shop has a familiar favourite he could collect today. The two offers cost the same in this exercise.

Fox: “Which offer would you put in front of him first?”

- POST: Send the three-film list by post.
- SHOP: Reserve the favourite at the shop.

Choose before viewing the next beat. This first prediction is unscored.

### FILM-B · Guests soon
Fox: “A week later, same Arun. This time two friends will arrive in an hour.”

Arun opens his three-film list, then closes it when he sees the postal arrival date: Tuesday. He checks the nearby shop. It has the familiar favourite; collection takes 20 minutes. His friends have already agreed to watch it. Both offers still cost the same. He has no other playable film at home.

Fox: “Which offer would you put in front of him now?”

- POST: Send the three-film list by post.
- SHOP: Reserve the favourite at the shop.

Choose before viewing the next beat. This prediction is also unscored.

## Guided Q&A

### JTBD-GA-04 · Why wait for the post?
Fox: “On Monday, was Arun getting out of an errand—or getting out of a film rut?”

- ERRAND: Make film night happen without a trip to the shop.
- DISCOVERY: Find something different from his usual viewing.

Pick the stronger explanation for Monday's choice, using what he did before choosing a service.

### JTBD-GB-04 · The awkward clue
Fox: “We think the approaching visit tipped Arun toward the shop. Which of these later choices would make you question that?”

In each case, the same film is available by both services at the same price. Arun has time to collect it. Pick the observation that challenges the timing explanation more.

- EARLY: With a week left, he collects it today rather than taking delivery tomorrow.
- LATE: With an hour left, he collects it today rather than taking delivery tomorrow.

## Quiz

### JTBD-QA-04 · The bags at six
Fox: “Leena receives takeaway at six. Her parents are visiting. The kitchen is clean. What would you put in your research notes?”

- TALK: Time with her parents is the reason for this order.
- OPEN: Both time together and a restaurant treat fit this order.
- TREAT: A restaurant treat is the reason for this order.

### JTBD-QB-04 · Watch the checkout
Fox: “Now you can see what happened before she paid. What was she protecting?”

Leena compared two orders. Both fed four, arrived at seven and cost the same. One was a new restaurant she had saved for months. The other was an ordinary meal requiring no serving or washing up. She removed the saved restaurant's order when she learned its dishes needed heating and plating. She bought the ordinary meal and put away the serving dishes.

- OCCASION: The chance to share a meal she'd been looking forward to.
- HOST: An evening without being responsible for work around the meal.

Choose the stronger reading of that tradeoff.

### JTBD-QC-04 · The next visit
Fox: “Next Friday, six people are coming. You're choosing the first offer Leena sees.”

Last Friday, three guests arrived at seven and three at eight. Leena's usual restaurant delivered all six meals at seven. The first three guests finished eating before the others arrived. The later guests reheated their meals; Leena kept leaving her seat to help. Everyone liked the food.

This Friday the same guests will arrive at the same two times. Both offers use the usual restaurant and the same six individual meals, at the same total price. Each meal arrives ready to eat. Leena can receive either delivery arrangement. Her past orders show she usually checks out in one visit.

- TOGETHER: Put “One delivery at seven” first: confirm once and receive all six meals.
- WAVES: Put “Deliver at seven and eight” first: confirm two slots and receive three meals each time.

Which would you put first to address what went wrong last Friday?
