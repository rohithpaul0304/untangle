# JTBD opening comparison — human test plan

Status: Superseded for this candidate set. Founder rejected all three openings. Newcomer participants: 0. Retained as a protocol template; do not recruit for these rejected decks without a new reason.

## First, compare the openings

Give the participant the comparison page without the designer notes or review. Across different people, rotate the starting candidate A/B/C. Do not explain JTBD or tell them which version is preferred.

Say: “Try this opening as you would on your phone. You can stop whenever you want.”

Record the first place they pause, reread, ask for context, misread a button, or stop. Record what they say verbatim. Do not explain the screen to keep them moving. If help is needed, record that assistance and do not count the rest as unaided understanding.

After an ending, hide the deck. Ask one question at a time:

1. “Why did Mira cancel? What does her five-star review tell you—and what does it not tell you?”
2. “What was she trying to get done before her interview, and what was getting in her way?”
3. “Why did the useful help change in the second situation?”
4. “Where would you stop if this were on your own phone?”
5. “Which opening would you continue: A, B, C, or none? What made the difference?”

Prompts 1–3 check understanding; 4–5 check experience and preference. A preference does not establish learning. Successful explanation after coaching is not an unaided pass.

## What counts as understanding

- They link cancellation to her present lack of preparation need, not automatically to dissatisfaction, nor to a universal rule that cancellations are good.
- They describe her desired outcome and concrete obstacle, rather than repeat a product name or the phrase “jobs to be done.”
- They explain why timed answer organisation helps the first obstacle and unseen-question practice helps the changed obstacle, without claiming either guarantees an offer.
- They recognise that the earlier preparation sequence is a reconstruction and the changed-obstacle sequence is a comparison, not evidence that a product caused her hiring.

Capture “unclear” where the explanation is missing. Do not turn one person’s result into an audience-wide learning claim.

## Next test after selecting a version

Use one new situation without interview wording, before giving feedback. Ask what the person is trying to accomplish, what blocks them, and what help would address that block. This transfer test belongs to the expanded lesson; these short openings alone cannot establish transfer or retention.
