# Jobs to Be Done — focused teaching brief

Status: Draft. Awaiting independent source and learning-design review.

## Purpose and boundaries

Approved hook: **“Five-star review. Subscription cancelled.”** The interview-practice app and all customer evidence below are fictional. This is a teaching specification, not a script or a prescribed interaction. Preserve designer freedom. The locked game-theory opening stays untouched.

Audience assumption: curious early-career people and product-management beginners, with no prior framework knowledge. This audience has not been validated.

**One capability:** Given a customer's behaviour or feature request and evidence about their circumstances, choose product help that addresses the progress they are trying to make, and explain why that help fits the evidenced obstacle.

Tomorrow's usable move: before recommending a feature, complete “In this situation, this person needs to get from ___ to ___; ___ is stopping them.” Tie the proposed help to that obstacle. If the evidence cannot fill a blank, identify what remains unknown instead of inventing a motive.

## Concept and mechanism

**Jobs to Be Done:** the progress a person seeks in particular circumstances, for which they might use a product or another solution. Here “job” means desired progress, not employment. It is neither an app activity such as “practise daily” nor a feature such as “a question bank.”

**Mechanism:** A person's circumstances make particular progress valuable and particular obstacles important, so useful product help depends on those circumstances rather than on the product category or the amount of use.

Concept kind: a product-design principle involving an unseen customer purpose. Do not select a play lens here.

**Tempting wrong model:** People buy an interview-practice app because they want more interview practice; giving a satisfied customer more practice should keep helping them. This mistake confuses the means with the progress it serves.

A narrower wrong conclusion also needs preventing: cancellation means the app failed—or, after this reveal, cancellation means it succeeded. Neither follows from the two opening facts.

## Evidence spine: what must become knowable

**Hook facts only:** Mira gave the app five stars and cancelled. These support several explanations. Any prediction made here must be unscored; “not enough information” is legitimate. Do not reward guessing the hidden story.

**Revealing fact:** Mira accepted a job offer and says she has no other interviews planned. She cancelled because she no longer needs preparation now. This establishes her stated reason in the fiction; it does not prove the app caused her hiring.

**The useful evidence behind that reveal:** Before the interview, Mira could describe her project accurately in a relaxed conversation. In rehearsal, her answer ran past the interview's stated two-minute limit and lost its main point. She worked late, so friends were rarely available when she could practise. She used the app privately at night to turn that experience into a clear spoken answer under the actual constraint. She reports that this helped her say what she meant without rambling.

The job supported by this evidence: **when an interview is approaching and live practice partners are unavailable, help me present my relevant experience clearly under interview constraints, so I can make a credible case for the role.** Getting hired is her larger aim, but an app cannot guarantee it. Private practice and feeling prepared matter here because her evidence establishes them, not because every job must tick three framework boxes.

**Teaching decision:** Which kind of help most directly addresses Mira's documented pre-interview obstacle? The learner's role is someone deciding what preparation help to provide; the goal is helping this customer perform the interview task. Their control is the help offered, not the employer's decision. The stake is spending her limited preparation time on the relevant obstacle. This is not a decision about pricing or retaining her after she cancels.

Before any graded response, disclose the full relevant evidence, what each available intervention does, and any time/cost constraints the eventual design uses. Useful help here supports practising and revising a concise spoken project answer within two minutes, alone at her available time. General topic lessons may help other customers but do not directly address the gap described. Do not turn this into a claim that one feature will improve company revenue or all users' outcomes.

**Consequence contract:** Show what the chosen help permits Mira to attempt or change, and what obstacle remains. Any simulated improvement must be a disclosed fictional assumption. Do not manufacture another job offer, a renewal, or a perfect performance as a guaranteed payoff.

## Second move: one changed obstacle

After naming the concept, use a clearly labelled comparison with the same interview, deadline, private practice constraint and desired progress. Change only the observed remaining obstacle: **she already delivers a clear answer within two minutes, but loses her thread when an interviewer asks an unexpected follow-up.** Give this evidence before the decision.

Repeating the first recommendation now misses the remaining gap. Help that lets her practise responding to varied follow-up questions fits more directly than further polishing the same prepared answer. This is a conditional reversal of the best-supported help, not a claim that concise answers have become worthless. The visible consequence must distinguish rehearsing a fixed answer from preparing to respond when the conversation changes.

## Fresh transfer: infer the job from a request

Use a parcel-delivery setting, with no cancellation, praise or interview. A home baker asks for a live courier map. Her evidence: she can package an order before leaving for her day job; pickup varies between 9 and 11; she leaves at 9:30; nobody can hand over the package afterward. Merely watching an approaching courier does not ensure handover.

The learner should infer the needed progress—hand over the order without missing work—and recommend help that addresses that conflict. An agreed pickup before 9:30 or a workable collection arrangement is supported **if its availability is stated**. A map alone leaves the evidenced obstacle unresolved. Accept equivalent feasible solutions with this reasoning; do not assume the requested feature is always wrong. Here the signal is a request, the constraint is coordination, and the solution can cross a product boundary.

## Fair scoring and fictional assumptions

- **Objectively checkable:** what the supplied evidence says; whether a proposal addresses the stated obstacle; whether the learner confuses app use with customer progress. Missing evidence permits uncertainty.
- **Conditional:** the best fit among specified interventions, given their disclosed capabilities and constraints. Credit equivalent reasoning and feasible alternatives. No single wording is required.
- **Tradeoffs:** what the company should build first, acceptable cost, pricing, and commercial impact. These need additional evidence and must not receive a universal right answer.

All people, histories, interview constraints and parcel details are authored assumptions. Do not present them as research findings. The lesson may demonstrate a fit relationship; it cannot establish population demand, actual learning, hiring causality or retention effects.

## Learning design

Misconception: product activity defines customer value. Predict moment: interpret the ambiguous hook without a score. Reveal: the customer's changed circumstances, then the evidence of what preparation was for. Invite an explanation before confirming and naming JTBD. Application: choose help for the evidenced obstacle. Second move: change the obstacle while preserving the job. Transfer: infer progress from a feature request in a coordination problem.

Load risks: keep the customer, time period and decision goal explicit; distinguish the historical preparation decision from cancellation today. Introduce no metrics lesson or taxonomy drill. Practice plan for a later phase: retrieve the situation–progress–obstacle relationship in varied cases, with an explained worked path after repeated difficulty and a later fresh check. No practice items are in this scope. Enjoyment, comprehension and delayed transfer remain untested.
