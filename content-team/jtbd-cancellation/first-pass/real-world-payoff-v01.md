# JTBD real-world closing application — proposed addendum

Status: Draft. Recommendation for independent review; not a founder-approved example choice.

## Recommended ending

Use **Netflix and Blockbuster** to extend version 0.2's discovery: a product category does not tell us what progress a customer wants. Mira was not pursuing endless interview practice; similarly, “rent a film” can conceal different needs. The circumstances explain different service designs.

Keep the history explicit: this is **Netflix's early-2000s DVD-by-mail period**, not today's streaming service. In HBS's explanatory account, Blockbuster served the immediate movie-night trip, while Netflix served planned discovery and viewing shaped around personal tastes. Stocking nearby shelves and organising a mailed catalogue can make sense for different circumstances. Mailing a DVD does not solve an evening's last-minute need; having a nearby rental store does not by itself solve discovering less obvious films over time. These are the article's contrasting customer situations, not claims about every customer or a complete explanation of either company's fortunes.

Two services in the same category need not help with the same progress. The lesson should finish with that practical implication, rather than with “Netflix won because it used JTBD.”

## Place in version 0.2

Preserve the accepted cancellation opening, historical reconstruction and changed-obstacle comparison. **Keep the parcel case as the independent transfer task.** Follow it with one brief Netflix/Blockbuster application as the real-world payoff before the final takeaway. This proposes an addition, not a replacement of the reviewed transfer or an extension of its review status. The five examples below are an optional bank, not five compulsory mini lessons.

The source story is an explanation, not evidence that a learner can apply the idea. If subsequently adapted into a scored decision, disclose the customer's timing, desired progress, available services and actual capabilities before asking for an inference. Grade the link between those facts and the proposed help, allowing feasible alternatives; never grade brand recall. Any invented customer, availability or service rule must be labelled as a teaching assumption, separate from the historical account.

## Compact example bank

- **Zoom:** The article presents remote colleagues needing to connect and work together without being physically together. Use it to separate the meeting tool from the coordination it serves.
- **PayPal:** The article connects online payment with ease and a sense of trust. Use it to show that feeling comfortable completing a transaction can matter alongside completing the payment.
- **DoorDash:** The article frames food delivery around obtaining food without leaving home. Use it to distinguish hunger relief under a constraint from wanting another ordering app.
- **Nike:** The article describes footwear designed around runners' needs. Use the design intent only; do not claim demonstrated injury prevention, performance gains or market superiority.
- **Netflix/Blockbuster:** Use the historical contrast above to show why a category label can hide different circumstances and desired progress.

## Practical exit and evidence boundary

Before proposing a feature tomorrow, write the triggering situation, the progress sought and the obstacle; then compare two feasible ways to help, including one outside the product when relevant. State which fact makes one fit better and what changed circumstance would reverse that judgment. Missing facts remain questions to investigate.

The founder's expectation that these examples may hook learners is a design hypothesis. Engagement and learning still need observation.
