# Why these three openings were rejected

Status: Superseded candidates; founder decision recorded 7 October 2026.

## Founder decision

“none” → “Reject all the openings” → “I want to check who came up with this scenario of an AI prep agent. It's bad. There's no hook”. All A/B/C candidates and their shared interview-prep premise are rejected. JTBD remains the topic. No winner, full-scene expansion or app integration is authorised by this rejection.

## Origin and responsibility

The earliest saved provenance located is the coordinator-authored `research/jtbd/hbs-examples.md`. Its “Implications for the approved direction” section already names the fictional interview-practice app. It expressly distinguishes that invention from the HBS cases. This establishes that the premise predates the Play Designers and was in the coordinator's planning input. The exact first chat sentence introducing it was not recovered in this audit.

The Subject Teacher, `/root/jtbd_teacher`, developed the fictional customer, interview constraints and help-choice cases in `first-pass/brief-v01.md`, then revised them into the shared brief. Separate source and teaching reviewers checked factual boundaries and learning logic; they did not validate audience interest.

The coordinator then made the cancellation/interview-prep scenario a fixed input in `designer-packet.md`. `/root/jtbd_play_a`, `/root/jtbd_play_b` and `/root/jtbd_play_c` independently designed interactions within that premise. They were not asked to compete on different real-world scenarios. Their independence was real, but constrained to presentation and interaction. Coordinator owns that framing and the decision to advance it.

## Diagnosis from the delivered work

- The cancellation mystery is answered after asking Mira. It creates little for the player to cause or discover through action.
- The lesson then rewinds into selecting rehearsal help. That second task is weaker than the opening promise and changes the focus from a surprising product insight to a narrow feature-fit exercise.
- Three lenses did not fix the shared weak premise. Different controls cannot create meaningful stakes on their own.
- Structural and browser checks proved that routes work, not that they deserve attention. Those checks do not override the rejection.

These are coordinator design findings, not measured audience claims. Preserve the Playtest Lead's limited cold observations separately; do not portray an interrupted review as a passed gate.

## Next step

Return to premise selection. Have the Teacher and independent Play Designers compare three genuinely different, source-grounded situations before another script or deck. For each, show the immediate decision, its visible consequence, and the non-obvious discovery. Use the supplied real-company cases as candidate material, not merely as a reward saved for the end. The choice of replacement remains open.
