## Blocker disposition

1. **Diagnostic evidence versus solved answer — resolved.** The brief explicitly withholds the job formulation, recommended fit and successful-use report until after the learner's inference. It requires the learner to connect circumstances, progress, obstacle and help. The hook is accurately called an unscored hypothesis, with uncertainty accepted. This closes the earlier risk under **“Payoff explained before the move”** and **“Grading an uninformed guess on a hidden rule.”**

2. **Unfixed causal demonstration — resolved.** Four consequence rows now settle what the selected help permits and what remains unresolved. Disclosed starting performance and stable help capabilities support both recommendations. The demonstration makes an opportunity available; it does not invent guaranteed training gains. Each alternative has a credible benefit, and combining them later is allowed. This addresses **“Explaining a change that is not shown,” “Forced actions dressed as decisions,”** and **“Arbitrary restriction to force the answer.”** The second move changes only the evidenced obstacle and reverses the best-supported first help.

3. **Unspecified time relationships — resolved.** The brief explicitly separates cancellation now, a hypothetical reconstruction of preparation, and an alternate preparation comparison. Their explanatory purposes are stated, and every response must be acknowledged. This supplies the missing content connection behind PILLARS' **“Why did the story jump?”** and **“A choice with no acknowledgment.”**

## Remaining design risks, not teaching blockers

- The target is reasoning about progress, not matching “timed answer” to “timed rehearsal.” The required explanation is essential. The parcel case is a modest transfer check, not a difficult puzzle or proof of deep understanding; a mere map-versus-guaranteed-pickup button choice would weaken it. Judge decks against **“Obvious advice in plain words,” “Prudent-versus-careless options,”** and **“Report editing and draggable fractions.”** The brief does not require that binary presentation.
- The three time frames create a load risk even with their meaning fixed. Designers must make the transitions readable without turning the lesson into an administrative exercise. Play review must assess the actual transitions; this review only checks their coherent specification.
- Scoring can establish evidence-based fit within the fictional constraints, not that the proposed intervention improves real performance. The current brief preserves this limit.
