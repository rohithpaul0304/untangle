# Jobs to Be Done — focused teaching brief, version 0.2

Status: Draft. Repair round 1; awaiting independent re-review.

## Purpose

Approved hook: **“Five-star review. Subscription cancelled.”** All customer details are fiction. No interaction or play lens is prescribed. The locked game-theory opening remains untouched.

Audience assumption, unvalidated: curious early-career people and product-management beginners; no prior framework knowledge.

**One capability:** Given customer behaviour or a feature request and evidence about the situation, choose help that addresses the progress the person seeks, explaining its fit to the evidenced obstacle.

Tomorrow's usable move: before recommending a feature, complete “In this situation, this person needs to get from ___ to ___; ___ is stopping them.” Connect help to that obstacle; identify unknowns rather than invent motives.

## Concept — author reference, confirmed after the learner's inference

**Jobs to Be Done:** progress a person seeks in particular circumstances, for which they might use a product or another solution. “Job” here means desired progress, not employment; “practise daily” is an activity, not necessarily that progress.

**Mechanism:** Circumstances make particular progress valuable and particular obstacles important, so useful help depends on the customer's situation rather than the product category or amount of use.

Concept kind: a product-design principle involving an unseen customer purpose.

**Tempting wrong model:** People buy an interview-practice app because they want more practice; more practice should therefore keep helping a satisfied customer. Also reject both blanket claims that cancellation proves failure and that cancellation proves success.

## Timeline and disclosure boundaries

1. **Actual fictional present:** Mira gave five stars and cancelled. Invite an unscored hypothesis about why; uncertainty is valid. This interprets known behaviour, not a prediction of an unseen result. Acknowledge the learner’s hypothesis without inventing their reasoning. Reveal Mira's account: she accepted an offer, has no further interviews planned, and no longer needs preparation now. That resolves her stated reason for leaving; it does not establish that the app caused hiring.
2. **Hypothetical reconstruction of the past:** Revisit what help would fit her documented situation before that interview. This explains what practice was serving; it neither offers preparation after cancellation nor rewrites the outcome.
3. **Alternate preparation comparison:** After confirming and naming the concept, change one obstacle in that earlier situation. This tests whether changed needs change the help; it is not another interview after cancellation.

Only the hook facts appear before the hypothesis. Before the preparation decision, disclose the evidence and help capabilities below. Keep the job formulation, recommended fit and reported successful use out of that evidence. The learner supplies **circumstance → sought progress → obstacle → suitable help** before confirmation. Acknowledge every decision.

## Preparation case: evidence available before the decision

Mira had an interview approaching. It included an opening project answer limited to two minutes, followed by questions. She wanted to explain why her experience suited the role. She could describe her project accurately in relaxed conversation, but her recorded rehearsal reached its main result only after the two-minute limit. She worked late; friends were rarely available when she could practise.

The learner's role: decide the **first preparation help** to provide. Goal: help Mira present her experience under the interview's constraints. Control: which help she receives first, not the employer's decision. Stake: using her available preparation time on the relevant obstacle. Both forms of help below work privately at her available time; no price or duration advantage is assumed. Combining them later is allowed.

**Disclosed capabilities, fixed across both cases:**

- **Structured rehearsal:** record the same project answer with a two-minute stop, replay it, organise its opening/evidence/result, and retry. It provides no unexpected follow-up questions.
- **Follow-up rehearsal:** respond to varied, previously unseen questions about the project and replay those responses. It provides neither a complete timed opening-answer attempt nor support for organising that answer.

Added interventions or constraints need review before affecting scoring.

## Consequences and answer boundary — author-facing

| Case and help | Observable opportunity the help provides | Remaining gap; credible benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Initial case: structured rehearsal | The authored starting answer stops before its main result; replay exposes that omission. Mira can reorganise and retry the same answer. | Responding to surprise questions remains untested. The relevant benefit is a direct opportunity to repair and test the evidenced timing/structure gap. |
| Initial case: follow-up rehearsal | A new follow-up appears; Mira can record and replay her response to it. | No complete opening answer has been revised and tested against its limit. This still offers useful practice responding beyond a prepared answer, but does not target the documented gap first. |
| Changed-obstacle case: structured rehearsal | The authored starting answer already delivers its main point within two minutes; another fixed-answer attempt is available. | No unexpected question occurs. Maintaining or refining the prepared answer is useful, but her response to a changed conversation remains untested. |
| Changed-obstacle case: follow-up rehearsal | An unseen follow-up requires a fresh response; that response becomes available for review and retry. | This exercises the evidenced gap; it does not prove she can handle other unfamiliar questions or the real interview. |

Fix these fictional observations before design; do not invent fluency gains. Starting recordings reflect disclosed evidence. The consequence demonstrates what becomes possible and what remains untested, not guaranteed learning or hiring. Learners may predict that opportunity; score fit from preceding evidence, not hidden performance.

**Post-response interpretation:** Mira needs to present relevant experience clearly under interview constraints when a practice partner is unavailable. Structured rehearsal fits the initial obstacle most directly; app activity is a means to that progress. Her report that private rehearsal helped her communicate can follow this reasoning, but is not evidence that the learner independently inferred it or that the app caused the offer.

## Second move: change only the remaining obstacle

Preserve the interview, deadline, availability and desired progress. Replace the performance evidence with: **Mira already gives a clear opening answer within two minutes, but loses her thread on an unexpected follow-up.** Disclose this before choosing help.

Follow-up rehearsal now addresses the remaining gap more directly. Repeating the first recommendation is not worthless, but no longer the best-supported first help. Use the fixed consequences above; confirm the reasoning after the response.

## Fresh transfer: request versus progress

**Pre-response evidence:** A home baker asks for a live courier map. Her parcel is ready at 9; ordinary pickup varies from 9 to 11; she must leave for her day job at 9:30; nobody can hand it over afterward. The map shows location but cannot change pickup. A guaranteed 9–9:20 pickup is available at the same cost. State all of this before scoring.

Require the learner to infer the progress and justify help before showing the interpretation. **Author key:** completing handover without missing work is the need; the guaranteed window addresses it. A map offers visibility but leaves the conflict unresolved. Credit another feasible arrangement with the same reasoning. Do not generalise that maps or customer requests are wrong. The signal, domain and constraint differ from the app case.

## Fair scoring and learning design

**Objective:** accurate use of supplied facts and intervention capabilities. **Conditional:** best fit to this person's obstacle under those facts; credit equivalent explanations and feasible alternatives. **Tradeoffs:** pricing, development priority and commercial impact need additional evidence and have no universal correct answer here.

Learning sequence: unscored hypothesis → reason for cancellation → reconstruction and learner inference → consequence and explanation → concept name → changed obstacle → fresh transfer. Keep historical reconstruction distinct from the present and alternate case. Avoid a metrics lesson or framework taxonomy.

Later practice should require retrieval across varied situations. Mark unsupported motives, app activity substituted for progress, and infeasible recommendations as **review-needed**, not mastery. After a second difficulty, provide an explained worked path followed by a fresh changed case; record supported and independent responses separately. A delayed fresh check is needed before claiming retention. No practice items are included here; enjoyment and learning remain untested.
