## Source coverage

The article frames product choice around the problem or opportunity customers are addressing in particular circumstances. It distinguishes functional, emotional and social dimensions. Its milkshake account describes commuters wanting hunger relief and something lasting through their drive; checkout speed and drink consistency follow from that situation.

Named examples: Zoom, PayPal, DoorDash, Nike and Netflix. The Netflix/Blockbuster comparison contrasts planned, personalized DVD discovery with an immediate rental for movie night. Treat this as the article's explanatory account, not proof of a single cause of business success. Historical figures, market rankings and executive titles need separate verification before reuse.

## Implications for the approved direction

Founder accepted JTBD as the topic and the hook direction “Five-star review. Subscription cancelled.” That interview-practice app is our fictional scenario, not a case from this article. The teaching target is the progress a customer seeks in a specific situation; cancellation is a possible clue, not the concept itself.

Do not infer successful job completion from a positive review or cancellation alone. Show evidence of the customer's situation and outcome before asking for a scored inference. Distinguish “this customer no longer needs interview preparation after getting hired” from “all cancellations are good.” Avoid drifting into a retention-metric lesson.

Candidate source use: milkshake case for a concrete application outside software; Netflix comparison for later contrast. Neither replaces the approved hook automatically. Keep source links in internal notes, not learner dialogue. No lesson script, deck or prototype authored in this intake.
