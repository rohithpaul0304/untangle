# Source map: IIMA Coursera course and The Art of Strategy

Captured 9 October 2026 from Rohith's Coursera dump of "Strategy and Game Theory for Management" (IIMA) and the recommended reading, Dixit and Nalebuff, The Art of Strategy (2008). PDF kept at references/the-art-of-strategy-dixit-nalebuff.pdf. Use as the spine for the Untangle game theory subject; do not copy wording from either.

## Coursera course, four weeks

Week 1, Why people don't cooperate: what a game is, simultaneous games, dominant strategy, prisoners' dilemma (tragedy of commons, drug prescribing in India, beauty, cigarette advertising ban), iterated elimination of dominated strategies, median voter theorem, level-k thinking, traveller's dilemma. Book chapters 1 and 3.

Week 2, Nash equilibrium: best response, Nash equilibrium, stag hunt (equilibrium is not always efficient), battle of the sexes (equilibrium is not always equal), hawk-dove and chicken (equilibrium is not always symmetric), focal points, coordination games, climate change, digital markets and single vs multi homing, inequality in equilibrium, mixed strategies (zero-sum, expected values, rock paper scissors, penalty kicks, Wimbledon, action bias). Book chapters 4 and 5.

Week 3, Sequential games: backward induction, centipede game, ultimatum game and behaviour, emotions, trust games, intellectual property and innovation, rare diseases, finitely repeated prisoners' dilemma, tit for tat, shadow of the future, what cooperation entails, cartels, Axelrod. Book chapters 2, 6, 7 and 9.

Week 4, Uncertainty in information: first vs second price sealed bid auctions, revenue equivalence, winner's curse (M&A), lemons, adverse selection, signalling (peacocks, education), screening and multiple contracts, insurance. Book chapters 8 and 10.

## The Art of Strategy, contents

Introduction: How should people behave in society?
Part I: 1 Ten tales of strategy. 2 Games solvable by backward reasoning. 3 Prisoners' dilemmas and how to resolve them. 4 A beautiful equilibrium.
Part II: 5 Choice and chance. 6 Strategic moves. 7 Making strategies credible.
Part III: 8 Interpreting and manipulating information. 9 Cooperation and coordination. 10 Auctions, bidding and contests. 11 Bargaining. 12 Voting. 13 Incentives. 14 Case studies. Plus workouts.

## Lecturer's teaching moves worth borrowing

Red man and blue man as the two players; "freeze the other player's strategy and ask: would you change?" as the definition of equilibrium; the diabetic hunter as a focal point; cricket vs opera; "equilibrium is not efficiency, equilibrium is not equality, equilibrium is not symmetry" as the three lessons of week 2; the live-class observation that most smart students still choose rabbit.
