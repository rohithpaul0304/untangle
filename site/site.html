Why play

Curious people used to learn everything by doing.

Leonardo learned anatomy by cutting, music by playing and engineering by building. Nobody handed him a syllabus. Then learning became reading about things, and now it is mostly scrolling past them.

Untangle brings the doing back. One idea becomes one small game. You poke it, it answers, and the idea sticks because your hands were on it. Game theory is the first subject. Behavioural economics, probability and everyday economics are next, built the same way.

How it works

Play first.
Names later.

Every lesson is the same shape, borrowed from how people actually learn games: you make a move, you watch what it does, you make a bigger one. The word for the idea arrives only after you have already used it.

Step 1

Make a move

Stay silent, or talk. One tap, and the night plays out in front of you.

Step 2

Watch it bite

The other player answers tomorrow. The move that won tonight costs you by morning.

Step 3

See it in the world

Same idea, real names. A price war, a law, a company that closed. You leave able to spot it around you.

Common questions

Is it free?

Chapter one is free, here, with no account and nothing to install. The app carries the rest of the course. If it ever costs money, we will tell you plainly before you pay.

Is this for kids?

It is built for adults who are curious and short on time: students, people in their first jobs, anyone who has started a course and not finished it. A sharp fourteen-year-old will be fine too.

How is this different from a YouTube video?

A video explains and you nod. Here you make the move, the world answers, and the idea arrives because you felt it. Nothing is named before you have used it.

Will I actually remember anything?

That is the point of playing instead of reading. Every chapter ends with a quick skill check and a real case, so you leave able to spot the idea around you, not just recognise its name.

Is it really game theory, though?

Yes, the real thing: the prisoner's dilemma, repeated games, tit for tat, Nash equilibrium. Then behavioural economics, probability and risk, and everyday economics, built the same way.
